AI Voice Agent for Inbound Sales: Turn Every Inquiry Into a Qualified Lead

An AI voice agent can ask the qualifying questions, but the part that decides what happens next should be ordinary code you can test. Here is how that works, with a routing layer I ran.

An AI voice agent qualifies an inbound call by asking your screening questions in conversation, turning the answers into structured fields, and passing them to rules you wrote. The rules decide whether to book a meeting, hand the lead to a person, decline politely or transfer the call. It works when those rules, not the model, make the decision.

Do AI Voice Agents Work for Qualifying Inbound Callers?

For asking the same questions every time and writing the answers down, yes, and that part is not hard. For deciding what a vague, partial or contradictory answer means, it depends on how you build it. A caller who says "I'm not sure about budget yet" is the common case, not an edge case, and what the system does with it decides whether the agent helps or books your sales team into bad meetings.

I do not have a measured conversion lift, answer rate or time saving to give you, and I would distrust any page that quotes one without naming the deployment, the period and how it counted. What I can show is where the logic goes wrong and how to test for it before a caller ever reaches the agent.

What Happens Between "Hello" and a Booked Meeting

Split the work into five steps. Only the first two involve a language model. The other three are ordinary software, and ordinary software can be tested without a phone.

Step Done by Testable without a phone?
1. Hold the conversation and ask the screening questionsVoice agent (speech-to-text, LLM, text-to-speech)No, needs real or recorded calls
2. Turn what was said into fields: need, budget range, timeline, region, name, phone, emailLLM extraction, after or during the callPartly, on saved transcripts
3. Decide the route: book, send to a person, decline, transfer now, ignoreYour rules, as codeYes
4. Write the lead to the CRM and offer calendar slotsWebhook handler and API callsMostly, with a sandbox account
5. Notify a human and show them what was saidWebhook handlerYes

Most "AI sales agent" pages describe step 1 and skip step 3. Step 3 is where a lead is wrongly booked, wrongly turned away or lost, and it is the step you control completely.

Write the Criteria Before You Write the Script

Before any prompt, answer two questions. What makes a lead qualified for you? What disqualifies one immediately? Then split every criterion into a hard fail, which ends the call politely, and a soft one, which sends the lead to a person. I use that split because an answer the caller did not give is not a pass and is not a fail. It is unknown, and unknown goes to a human.

The table below is the example I used in the test. The business is invented and generic: it serves callers in the US and Canada, will not take work under $20,000, can start within 12 weeks, and offers three services.

Field the agent captures Hard fail (decline politely) Soft: unknown or doubtful (send to a person)
RegionOutside US and CanadaNever asked
What they needSomething you do not offerNever said
Budget rangeWhole range below $20,000Not given, range straddles $20,000, or two stated ranges that contradict
Start timelineNoneNot given, or beyond 12 weeks
Name and phoneNoneName missing or phone not valid
Asked for a personNot a fail: overrides everything and transfers or books a callback

A booking requires every row to be a clear pass. Anything else falls to a person or a polite decline. That one rule is the whole design.

The Routing Layer I Tested

Here is the core of it. budget_state reduces everything the caller said about money to one of five states, and route applies the table above.

def budget_state(budgets, minimum):
    """'ok' | 'below' | 'straddle' | 'unknown' | 'conflict'"""
    if not budgets:
        return "unknown"
    lo = max(b[0] for b in budgets); hi = min(b[1] for b in budgets)
    if lo > hi:
        return "conflict"                      # stated ranges do not overlap
    if hi < minimum:
        return "below"
    return "ok" if lo >= minimum else "straddle"

def route(x: Extracted, office_open: bool, criteria=CRITERIA):
    if x.is_solicitation:
        return "no_action", "caller is selling to us"
    if x.asked_for_human:
        return ("transfer_now", "caller asked for a person") if office_open \
            else ("callback_task", "caller asked for a person, office closed")
    if x.region is not None and x.region not in criteria["regions"]:
        return "decline_polite", f"region {x.region} not served"
    if x.need == "other":
        return "decline_polite", "need not offered"
    b = budget_state(x.budgets, criteria["min_budget"])
    if b == "below":
        return "decline_polite", "budget below minimum"
    soft = []
    if b != "ok": soft.append(f"budget {b}")
    if x.region is None: soft.append("region unknown")
    if x.need is None: soft.append("need unknown")
    if x.start_weeks is None: soft.append("timeline unknown")
    elif x.start_weeks > criteria["max_start_weeks"]: soft.append("timeline beyond capacity window")
    if not (x.name and E164.match(x.phone)): soft.append("contact incomplete")
    return ("book", "all criteria met") if not soft else ("human_review", "; ".join(soft))

The six cases the naive version gets wrong are all the same kind of mistake. A budget range that straddles the minimum, a caller who said "not sure", a caller who never gave a name, two budgets that contradict each other, no start date and no region all became bookings. Nobody wrote a rule that says "book unknowns". The naive code just never asked what happens when the field is empty. That is why I treat unknown as its own state instead of defaulting it to a pass.

What this does not show: the 14/14 is by construction, because I wrote the expected route and the router myself, so it checks the code against my policy and not against ground truth. The cases are synthetic. And it says nothing about step 2. How often an LLM extracts the right budget from a real accent-heavy phone call is a separate measurement, and the harder one. The method for that, with reviewed expected answers and failure traces, is in my post on how to evaluate an AI agent before deployment.

Handing Off to a Person

Two rules in the test do the work. A caller who asks for a person overrides every other rule. And if nobody is available, the route is a callback task, not a transfer into a dead line.

On the platform side, both vendors I checked document phone transfer. In Retell's docs, the Transfer Call tool works on phone calls only, not web calls, and offers cold, warm and agentic warm transfer. A warm transfer keeps the agent on the line after it dials the destination, so it can wait for a real person and play a message only that person hears. In Vapi's docs, the transfer call tool routes an active call to a phone number or SIP URI, and if neither the tool nor the destination sets a message the assistant says "Transferring the call now". Vapi also sends a status-update with status forwarding when a transfer starts, and its docs say that event does not confirm the destination rang, answered or accepted the caller. So log a transfer as attempted, not completed, and have a fallback when nobody picks up. I read both pages on 3 November 2026 and did not test either transfer.

Getting the Result Into Your CRM and Calendar

The voice platform tells your server the call is over, and your server does the rest. Two details from the docs matter more than the rest.

  • When the data arrives. Retell fires call_ended first, and that payload does not include call_analysis. The structured fields you define under post-call extraction arrive in the later call_analyzed webhook. Vapi's end-of-call-report carries the transcript, the messages and recording URLs.
  • Redelivery. Retell waits 10 seconds for a 2xx response and retries up to 3 times, so the same event can reach you more than once. Its docs recommend keying on the event name plus the call ID, and replying with a 2xx as soon as the payload is safely stored. The webhook class in my test does exactly that, and one test sends the same event twice and checks that only one CRM write happens.

For HubSpot, the contacts guide documents POST /crm/v3/objects/contacts/batch/upsert, where you set idProperty to email and existing contacts are updated while new ones are created. It also names email as the primary unique identifier for avoiding duplicate contacts, and notes that partial upserts are not supported when email is the idProperty. I did not test what that last note means against a live portal, so check it before relying on upsert to update an existing contact. The consequence for the voice call is practical: a caller who never gives a valid email cannot be matched by that key. In my design, that call creates a contact through the plain create endpoint and carries a flag asking a person to check for a duplicate. The test checks that a malformed address like "sam at example" is never used as the key. For the full integration, see how an AI voice agent connects to HubSpot.

For the calendar, Google's freeBusy.query (POST https://www.googleapis.com/calendar/v3/freeBusy) returns busy intervals for the calendars you list, and accepts narrow scopes including calendar.freebusy. That is enough to offer real open slots without giving the agent access to event details. Booking is a separate step with its own permission, and I would keep that step behind your rules too. Setting Up a First Inbound Qualification Agent

  1. Write the criteria table first. Hard fails, soft unknowns, and what a person does with each soft case. Ask whoever answers sales calls today what they actually ask.
  2. Write the router and its test table. Include the ugly cases: no answer, contradiction, a vendor pitching you, a caller who wants a human at 11 p.m.
  3. Pick a platform. Whether you build on a platform or have one managed for you depends on how custom the conversation and integrations need to be. I run Voxdonna, so I am not neutral; its voice agent page is the managed option, and the comparison post above is the neutral one.
  4. Define the extraction fields on the platform and map them to the fields the router expects. Mismatched names here silently turn everything into "unknown".
  5. Test with recorded and live calls before launch. Call it yourself and try to break it: partial answers, a changed mind, a request for a person.
  6. Review the first batch of real calls yourself. That is my recommendation, not a documented requirement. Compare what was said with what the extraction captured and what route it took.

What to Measure in the First 90 Days

Instead of promising results, here is what I would track, because each number points to a specific fix:

  • Answer rate: calls answered by the agent against calls that rang out. Take it from your phone provider's logs, not the voice platform's dashboard alone.
  • Extraction accuracy: on a weekly sample, did the captured budget, timeline and region match what the caller said? This is the number my routing test cannot give you.
  • Human-review share: the proportion of calls routed to a person. Very high usually means a question is being skipped or the criteria are too tight. Near zero usually means unknowns are being treated as passes.
  • Book rate and show rate: of the leads booked, how many reach the meeting, and how many your reps call qualified afterwards.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an AI voice agent actually close sales?

In my view it is better suited to qualifying and booking than to closing. A higher-value or considered purchase usually needs a person for the decision, so the agent's job is to capture the answers, book the next step and hand over with the notes already written. Simple, low-consideration purchases are a different case, and I have not tested those here.

How does an AI voice agent qualify a sales lead?

It asks your screening questions in conversation, converts the answers into structured fields such as need, budget range, timeline and region, then applies rules you wrote. Clear passes get a booking, hard fails get a polite decline, and anything unknown or contradictory goes to a person.

What happens when the caller wants a person?

The request should override every other rule. Retell and Vapi both document transferring a live phone call to a number or SIP URI, and Retell offers cold, warm and agentic warm transfer. Plan a callback task for when nobody is available, and treat a transfer as attempted until you can confirm someone answered.

Can it write to my CRM without creating duplicates?

With HubSpot, the documented route is the batch upsert endpoint with email as the idProperty, since email is the primary unique identifier. A caller who gives no valid email cannot be matched that way, so flag that contact for a person to check. Also make the webhook handler idempotent, because Retell can redeliver an event up to 3 times.

How much does an AI voice agent for inbound sales cost?

It depends on the platform, the call minutes and the setup work, and prices change often. I do not quote a single figure here. The dated per-minute prices and a worked model are in my pricing breakdown, and the ROI calculator takes your own call volume.
