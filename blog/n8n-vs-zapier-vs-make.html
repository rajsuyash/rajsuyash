n8n vs Zapier vs Make: Which Automation Platform Is Best in 2026?

n8n, Zapier and Make solve the same problem at three very different prices. n8n is open source: self-host it for the cost of a server, or use n8n Cloud from €20/month for 2,500 executions. Zapier has by far the largest app library and the highest bill, with Professional starting at $19.99/month billed annually for 750 tasks. Make sits between them at $9/month for 5,000 credits. Choose n8n for complex or AI-heavy workflows and cost control, Zapier for the widest app coverage with zero setup, Make for visual workflows on a small budget. Full pricing and feature detail below.

Updated September 2026: every price and integration count on this page was re-checked against the vendors' own pricing pages. Zapier has retired its Starter plan, Make has replaced per-operation billing with credits and consolidated Core, Pro and Teams into a single Make Plan, and n8n now prices its cloud tiers in euros.

N8N: The Developer's Choice

n8n is an open-source workflow automation tool, popular with developers, agencies and any business that wants full control over its automations.

Strengths

  • Self-hosting option. Run N8N on your own server for free. No per-execution limits. No vendor lock-in. Your data stays on your infrastructure.
  • Full code access. Write JavaScript or Python directly inside any workflow. This is critical for AI automation, data transformation, and custom API integrations.
  • Complex branching logic. If/else nodes, loops, merge nodes, error handling. N8N handles multi-step, conditional workflows that would require workarounds on other platforms.
  • AI-native workflows. Connect directly to OpenAI, Anthropic, local LLMs, or any AI API. Build AI agents that reason, decide, and act within a single workflow.

Weaknesses

  • Smaller app library. Roughly 2,100 integrations against Zapier's 10,000+. For anything less common you fall back to the HTTP Request node, which works but is more setup.
  • Steeper learning curve. N8N rewards technical users. If your team has no coding experience, the initial setup takes longer.
  • Self-hosting requires maintenance. You need to manage server updates, backups, and uptime. (N8N Cloud eliminates this, but adds cost.)

Pricing

  • Community Edition (self-hosted): free. You pay only for the server, typically $5 to $20/month. Our Docker self-hosting guide walks through the setup.
  • n8n Cloud Starter: €20/month for 2,500 workflow executions, 5 concurrent executions, unlimited users.
  • n8n Cloud Pro: €50/month for 10,000 executions, 20 concurrent executions, workflow history and execution search.
  • Business: €667/month for 40,000 executions, and the tier where SSO, Git version control and the supported self-hosted option appear.
  • Enterprise: custom pricing, with log streaming, external secret stores and an SLA.

Zapier: The Easy Button

Zapier pioneered the "if this, then that" approach to connecting apps and remains the easiest platform to start with.

Strengths

  • Massive app library. Over 10,000 integrations. If an app exists, Zapier probably connects to it. This is Zapier's biggest advantage and it is significant.
  • Dead-simple setup. Non-technical users can build automations in minutes. The interface is clean and the trigger/action model is intuitive.
  • Managed reliability. Uptime, scaling and error monitoring are Zapier's problem, not yours, and its docs and template library are the best in the category.
  • Tables and Interfaces. Zapier has added database-like features (Tables) and simple form builders (Interfaces) that extend its usefulness.

Weaknesses

  • Expensive at scale. Zapier charges per task, so cost tracks step count, not workflow count. The 10,000-task Professional tier is $129/month billed annually and $193.50 billed monthly; the same volume on self-hosted n8n is a server bill.
  • Limited logic. Complex branching, loops, and error handling are possible but clunky. Zapier works best for straightforward A-to-B automations.
  • No self-hosting. Your data runs through Zapier's servers. For regulated industries or privacy-conscious businesses, this can be a dealbreaker.
  • Limited custom code. Zapier's code steps exist but are restricted in execution time, libraries, and capability compared to N8N.

Pricing

Zapier retired the old Starter tier. The paid ladder is now Professional, Team and Enterprise, and every tier is priced by task volume. Monthly-billed prices are 50% higher than the annual rates shown first:

  • Free: 100 tasks/month, two-step Zaps.
  • Professional: from $19.99/month annual ($29.99 monthly) for 750 tasks. 2,000 tasks costs $49 annual ($73.50 monthly); 10,000 tasks costs $129 annual ($193.50 monthly).
  • Team: from $69/month annual ($103.50 monthly) for 2,000 tasks, adding shared workspaces and collaboration.
  • Enterprise: custom pricing, not purchasable online.

Make (formerly Integromat): The Middle Ground

Make sits between Zapier's simplicity and n8n's power: a visual, drag-and-drop builder with more flexibility than Zapier at a lower price.

Strengths

  • Visual workflow builder. The canvas interface shows your whole automation as a map, which helps when scenarios get complex.
  • Better pricing than Zapier. Make bills in credits, one per module step, but the cost per step is far below Zapier's per-task rate.
  • Solid integration count. Over 3,000 apps cover most popular tools. Not as extensive as Zapier, but enough for most businesses.
  • Good error handling. Make includes built-in error routes, retry logic, and data transformation tools that Zapier lacks.

Weaknesses

  • Confusing credit counting. Every action, filter and router consumes a credit. A five-step scenario can burn 5 to 10 credits per run, which makes cost estimation awkward. The 2026 switch from operations to credits also invalidates older cost comparisons.
  • No self-hosting. Like Zapier, Make is cloud-only. No option to run it on your own infrastructure.
  • Limited custom code. Make supports HTTP modules and some scripting, but it is not comparable to writing full JavaScript/Python in N8N.
  • Steeper than Zapier. The visual builder is powerful but takes more time to learn than Zapier's linear interface.

Pricing

Make replaced operations with credits as its billing unit in 2026, and collapsed the old Core, Pro and Teams tiers into one plan:

  • Free: 1,000 credits/month, 2 active scenarios, 15-minute minimum interval between runs.
  • Make Plan: from $9/month for 5,000 credits, with unlimited active scenarios. Larger credit bundles cost proportionally more.
  • Company: custom pricing, arranged with sales.

Because the unit changed, any Make cost estimate written before 2026 is no longer comparable. Rebuild your estimate from the credit count your scenarios actually consume.

What It Actually Costs at 10,000 Runs a Month

This is where the three diverge hardest. At 10,000 runs per month, using each vendor's published rates:

  • Self-hosted n8n: the server bill, nothing else. A $10 to $20 VPS handles this comfortably.
  • n8n Cloud Pro: €50/month, which is the tier that includes 10,000 executions.
  • Zapier Professional: $129/month billed annually, or $193.50 billed monthly, for the 10,000-task tier.
  • Make: credit-based, starting from the $9/month Make Plan and rising with the credit bundle you buy.

Two things distort any direct comparison, and both matter. A Zapier task is one action step, so a five-step Zap burns five tasks per run; a Make credit is consumed per module the same way; an n8n execution is one whole workflow run regardless of how many nodes it contains. That accounting difference, not the headline price, is what makes n8n cheap at volume and Zapier expensive. Before you compare, count how many steps your average workflow has and multiply.

The takeaway holds regardless: self-hosted n8n is the cheapest option at every scale, Zapier is the most expensive, and Make lands in between. For a longer breakdown of the migration maths, see our n8n cost savings analysis.

Which Platform Should You Choose?

Choose N8N if...

  • You are building complex, multi-step workflows with conditional logic.
  • You need AI automation (LLM chains, AI agents, custom AI pipelines).
  • You want to control costs at volume, or keep data on your own servers.
  • Your team has at least one person comfortable with technical concepts.
  • You are an AI automation agency building workflows for clients.

Choose Zapier if...

  • You need simple, straightforward automations (less than 5 steps).
  • Your team is non-technical and needs the fastest possible setup.
  • You rely on niche apps that only Zapier supports.
  • You run low volumes and value setup speed over long-term cost.

Choose Make if...

  • You want a visual workflow builder with more power than Zapier.
  • Budget matters, but you do not want to self-host.
  • Your scenarios are moderately complex and need better error handling than Zapier offers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is N8N really free?

Yes. n8n Community Edition is open source and free to self-host, with no per-execution fee and no cap on workflows or users. Your only cost is the server, typically $5 to $20/month on DigitalOcean or Hetzner. If you would rather not run infrastructure, n8n Cloud Starter is €20/month for 2,500 executions.

Can I migrate from Zapier to N8N?

Yes, but there is no one-click migration tool. You rebuild each Zap as an n8n workflow. Our Zapier to n8n migration guide covers the order to do it in. For complex multi-step Zaps, an automation expert can migrate and usually improve the workflows in the same pass.

Which platform is best for AI automation workflows?

n8n is the strongest choice. It has JavaScript and Python code nodes, direct API calls to any model, and the branching logic AI workflows need, so you can build full AI agents that reason, plan and use tools. Make handles moderate AI work through its HTTP modules. Zapier suits simpler AI tasks like text generation or summarization.
