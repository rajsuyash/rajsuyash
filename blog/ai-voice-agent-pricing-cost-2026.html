AI Voice Agent Pricing Guide 2026: Full Cost Breakdown for Small Businesses

Per-minute rates, packaged plans, setup time and human-staff costs from vendor pages read on 16 October 2026, plus a cost model you can re-run before you sign anything.

Short answer: An AI voice agent costs about $0.08 to $0.17 a minute on a self-serve platform once you add the model, voice and phone line, or from $29 to $349 a month on a packaged plan. At 300 three-minute calls a month that is roughly $74 to $349, against $2,100 for a human answering plan. As of 16 October 2026.

The most common question I get from small business owners researching AI voice agents is: "How much is this actually going to cost me?" The honest answer has three parts: a usage rate every vendor publishes, a plan fee some publish, and a setup cost nobody publishes. This guide prices all three from the vendors' own pages and ends with a short script you can re-run when the prices move.

The Four Ways to Buy an AI Voice Agent

The way you buy has a larger effect on your total cost than any single line item, because it decides who pays for the build and the tuning.

1. Self-Serve Platforms

You build the agent yourself on Vapi, Retell or Bland and pay per minute, with a plan fee on some tiers. None of the three pricing pages I read lists a setup fee. The cost is the hours you spend on prompts, testing and integrations, which no pricing page can publish.

2. Packaged AI Receptionist Plans

Dialzara and Goodcall sell a configured answering agent for a flat monthly fee. Dialzara counts talk-time minutes. Goodcall counts unique customers and says it never meters minutes. Dialzara's Plus and Elite tiers include ongoing custom prompt engineering; its Lite and Pro tiers give you a self-editable prompt. These plans suit a single line with standard flows, and they have feature limits that a platform build does not (Goodcall's Starter plan allows 5 workers and 1 external tool, for example).

3. Custom Build by a Consultant

I could not find a published price for this model, and I will not invent one. What I can say is which inputs a fair scope prices: minutes per month, concurrent calls, the systems the agent must read and write (calendar, CRM, ticketing), languages, and what happens when the agent hands a call to a person. Those are the same inputs the vendors above price on. If a quote does not break down along those lines, ask why.

4. Enterprise Platforms

Synthflow's pricing page now shows one tier: "Enterprise contracts start at $30,000 annually," scoped around call volume, concurrency, telephony setup, integrations and security. That is a different buyer from a small business.

The headline rates are not comparable. Vapi's $0.05 covers hosting alone, Retell's range includes its voice infrastructure, and Bland's rate bundles the model layer. To compare them I turned each page into a monthly number for the same workload.

Dialzara's per-minute overage makes it the most expensive AI option at 3,000 minutes. # Inputs are published prices read on 16 Oct 2026 (sources named inline).
# CALLS and MIN_PER_CALL are my assumptions, not measurements.

def self_serve(minutes):
    # Vapi: its own calculator shows $82-$129 per 1,000 min (hosting $0.05 + STT + LLM + voice, telephony $0)
    vapi = (minutes * 0.082, minutes * 0.129)
    # Retell: infra 0.055 + TTS + LLM + telephony 0.015 (page rates)
    retell_lo = 0.055 + 0.015 + 0.0064 + 0.015   # platform voice, GPT 5.4 nano
    retell_hi = 0.055 + 0.040 + 0.064 + 0.015    # ElevenLabs Flash, recommended LLM tier
    retell = (minutes * retell_lo, minutes * retell_hi)
    # Bland: LLM/STT/TTS bundled; telephony not priced on the page, so excluded
    bland_start = minutes * 0.14
    bland_build = minutes * 0.12 + 299
    return {"Vapi": vapi, "Retell": retell, "Bland Start": (bland_start,) * 2, "Bland Build": (bland_build,) * 2}

def dialzara(minutes):
    plans = [(29, 60, .48), (99, 220, .45), (199, 500, .40), (349, 1000, .35)]
    return min(b + max(0, minutes - inc) * o for b, inc, o in plans)

def goodcall(calls):
    cust = calls / 1.5   # Goodcall's own ratio: 100 customers ~ 150 calls
    plans = [(79, 100, .79), (129, 500, .26), (299, 2000, .15)]
    return min(b + max(0, cust - inc) * o for b, inc, o in plans)

def smith(calls):
    plans = [(300, 30, 11.5), (810, 90, 10.5), (2100, 300, 8.5)]
    return min(b + max(0, calls - inc) * o for b, inc, o in plans)

def ruby(minutes):
    plans = [(250, 50), (395, 100), (720, 200), (1725, 500)]
    ok = [b for b, inc in plans if minutes <= inc]
    return min(ok) if ok else None

MIN_PER_CALL = 3
print("Monthly cost by volume (USD). Assumes %d min per call." % MIN_PER_CALL)
for calls in (150, 300, 1000):

    for k, (lo, hi) in self_serve(m).items():
        print("  %-12s $%7.0f - $%7.0f" % (k, lo, hi))
    print("  %-12s $%7.0f" % ("Dialzara", dialzara(m)))
    print("  %-12s $%7.0f" % ("Goodcall", goodcall(calls)))
    print("  %-12s $%7.0f   (human receptionists, per call)" % ("Smith.ai", smith(calls)))
    r = ruby(m)
    print("  %-12s %s   (human receptionists, per minute)" % ("Ruby", "$%7.0f" % r if r else "above the 500-minute plan"))

print("\nReceptionist employee: BLS median wage May 2025 = $38,010 a year (wages only)")
ratio = 46.89 / 32.82

vapi_lo = self_serve(900)["Vapi"][0]
retell_hi = self_serve(900)["Retell"][1]
for hours in (20, 40, 80):
    for rate in (75, 150):
        setup = hours * rate
        print("  %2dh x $%3d = $%6.0f setup | year 1 = $%6.0f - $%6.0f" % (hours, rate, setup, setup + 12 * vapi_lo, setup + 12 * retell_hi))

for hours, rate in ((20, 75), (40, 75), (80, 150)):
    saving = smith(300) - self_serve(900)["Retell"][1]
    print("  setup $%6.0f, monthly saving up to $%.0f -> %.1f months" % (hours * rate, saving, hours * rate / saving))

What Drives the Cost Up

Outbound Calling

Outbound campaigns add line items. Retell lists $0.005 per dial for batch calls and $0.10 per outbound call for branded caller ID, and a verified phone number costs $10 per month. Outbound also brings consent and calling-hour rules that depend on where you call, which no pricing page prices for you.

Voice Choice and Cloning

Voice is a per-minute line on Retell's page: $0.015 for its platform voices, $0.04 for ElevenLabs Flash, up to $0.10 for ElevenLabs v3. If you want a cloned voice, ElevenLabs lists Instant Voice Cloning from its Starter plan ($6 a month, with a discounted first month) and Professional Voice Cloning from Creator ($22 a month). Check whether your platform bills the voice provider separately.

Languages and Integrations

None of the pricing pages I read lists a per-language fee, so ask for it in writing. Integrations are where scoped quotes diverge: reading a Google Calendar is a small job, and writing to a multi-location CRM or a proprietary database is not. Get each system named in the quote.

Concurrency and Compliance

Calls that overlap need lines. Vapi's usage-only tier includes 4 concurrent calls and charges $10 per extra line per month; Retell includes 20 and charges $8. Vapi also lists a HIPAA add-on at $2,000 per month on top of any plan, which matters if you handle health information.

High Call Volume

Usage cost is close to linear. 3,000 minutes a month on Vapi or Retell is $246 to $522 in the model above, before plan fees. The nonlinear parts are the plan tiers and overage: Bland Build's $299 fee, Vapi Pro's $999 monthly minimum, and Dialzara's overage rate.

How to Calculate Your Own Number

  1. Count minutes, not calls. Monthly minutes = calls per month times average minutes per call. Pull both from your phone provider's call log, not from memory.
  2. Price the all-in minute. On a platform, add hosting, speech-to-text, model, voice and telephony. On a packaged plan, find the billing unit (minutes or unique customers) and pick the tier that covers your volume.
  3. Add fixed fees. Plan fee, extra concurrency lines, phone numbers and any compliance add-on.
  4. Add setup and tuning time. Hours times your rate, for year 1, and a smaller number for each later month.
  5. Compare with what you pay now. Your receptionist or answering service bill, plus an honest estimate of calls that currently go unanswered.

Red Flags When Evaluating Vendors

  • A per-minute rate with no stack behind it. Vapi's own page says its $0.05 is hosting and that models are billed on top. Ask any vendor what the headline includes.
  • No scoping before a firm price. A quote that ignores your call volume, use cases and integrations is a guess or a template.
  • No uptime commitment in writing. Pages differ: Vapi lists 99% on Pro and 99.9% on Premier, and Bland lists 99.9% across plans. A free tier may list none.
  • No call recordings or analytics. If you cannot hear how the agent performs, you are flying blind. Dialzara lists recordings and call summaries on every plan. See our guide to AI voice agent analytics for what to track.
  • A long lock-in with no performance clause. Dialzara states no contract and Smith.ai states month-to-month billing. A 12-month commitment with no exit is worth pushing back on.

Questions to Ask Before You Sign

  1. What is the billing unit (seconds, minutes, calls, or unique customers), and does it include silence and transfers?
  2. What is included in the monthly fee, and what triggers overage?
  3. Who owns the conversation scripts and configurations if I leave?
  4. How long does setup take from signed contract to live agent?
  5. How are integrations priced? Is my CRM connection included or extra?
  6. What happens when call quality degrades? Who do I contact and how fast do you respond?

Add one more question about speed, and be specific: the compilation of published voice agent latency figures shows that every vendor defines the word differently, so ask what their number measures and at which percentile.

The Bottom Line on AI Voice Agent Pricing

At 300 three-minute calls a month, the published options in this guide come to $74 to $157 a month in usage on self-serve platforms, $129 to $349 on packaged plans, and $2,100 on a human answering plan. The self-serve figure leaves out your setup hours, which move year 1 from about $2,400 to about $13,900 on the assumptions above. Vendor prices change often, so re-run the script before you budget.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an AI voice agent cost per month?

At 300 three-minute calls a month, usage on Vapi or Retell comes to about $74 to $157, packaged plans such as Dialzara and Goodcall come to $129 to $349, and Bland is $126 to $407 depending on plan. These come from vendor pricing pages read on 16 October 2026 and my cost model; they exclude your setup hours.

Do AI voice agents charge per minute or per month?

Both exist. Vapi, Retell and Bland charge per minute, with plan fees on some tiers. Dialzara charges a monthly fee that includes minutes, then a per-minute overage. Goodcall charges a monthly fee per band of unique customers and does not meter minutes. Per-minute suits low or uneven volume; a flat plan suits steady volume that fits a tier.

How do I calculate the total monthly cost of AI inbound call automation?

Multiply calls per month by average minutes per call. Multiply that by the all-in per-minute rate (hosting, speech-to-text, model, voice, telephony), or pick the plan tier that covers it. Then add plan fees, extra concurrency lines and compliance add-ons, and spread your setup hours over the first year.

Is there a setup fee for an AI voice agent?

The platform and packaged-plan pricing pages I read list no setup fee. The real setup cost is the time you spend building and tuning the agent, or a builder's fee if you hire one, and I could not find published prices for custom builds. Ask for a scoped quote that names minutes, integrations, languages and handoff rules.

Which voice AI platform is cheapest per minute?

It depends on the stack. Vapi's own calculator shows $0.082 to $0.129 per minute, Retell comes to $0.091 to $0.174 in my model, and Bland lists $0.12 to $0.14 with the model bundled but telephony extra. On an identical model stack, Vapi and Retell land within a twentieth of a cent of each other, so concurrency fees and volume decide.

How does AI voice agent pricing compare with a receptionist or answering service?

A receptionist's median wage is $38,010 a year (BLS, May 2025), about $54,300 loaded by my estimate. Smith.ai's published plan for 300 calls a month is $2,100. An AI agent at the same volume is $74 to $349 a month before setup, but the options differ in coverage and in how many calls the agent finishes without a person.
